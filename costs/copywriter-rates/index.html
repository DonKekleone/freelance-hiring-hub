Freelancer Rates

What does a freelance copywriter cost?

Copywriting rates depend on the commercial importance of the work, research requirements, specialization and project scope.

Copywriter pricing depends on the job the words are doing

A 200-word landing page can require more commercial thinking than a 2,000-word informational article.

The useful pricing question is not only how many words are being written. It is what the copy is expected to accomplish.

Word count measures volume, not difficulty. Commercial copy is often expensive because of research, positioning and decision-making rather than typing time.

Deliverable type changes the pricing logic

Different copy assets involve different levels of research, strategy, coordination and commercial consequence.

Landing pages
Sales pages
Email sequences
Product descriptions
Website copy
Advertising copy
Case studies
Long-form content

A buyer should compare quotes for the same kind of deliverable rather than treating all writing as interchangeable.

Research depth is a major cost driver

Some assignments begin with a complete brief and source material. Others require the copywriter to interview stakeholders, review competitors, understand customers and investigate the product before drafting.

The research burden should be visible in the quote.

Customer research
Stakeholder interviews
Competitor review
Product or service research
Existing analytics or performance review
Source-material synthesis

Strategy can be separate from writing

A copywriter may be asked only to execute an existing message, or they may be expected to help define positioning, offer structure, page flow and message hierarchy.

Those are different scopes and should not be compared as if they were the same writing job.

Subject complexity affects time and specialization

Writing about a familiar consumer product is different from writing about a technical, regulated or highly specialized subject.

The more background knowledge required, the more time may be spent learning before the first draft is produced.

Commercial importance can affect the level of work required

Copy used in a high-traffic sales funnel or major launch may justify more research, review and iteration than low-stakes internal content.

That does not mean price should be based only on how valuable the business is. It means the buyer should recognize that higher-consequence assets often demand more rigorous work.

Per-word pricing can be useful for some content and weak for sales copy

Per-word pricing can make sense when output volume is closely related to the work required.

It becomes less useful when the assignment depends heavily on research, positioning or compression. A strong headline may take more thinking than several paragraphs of routine prose.

Project pricing can align better with a defined commercial deliverable

A project fee can make comparison easier when the buyer can define the asset, inputs, research requirements and revision process.

The buyer should still inspect what the project price includes.

Research
Messaging strategy
Number of drafts
Revision rounds
Meetings or interviews
Final formatting or implementation support

Hourly pricing can fit exploratory work

Hourly billing can make sense when the work is difficult to define in advance, especially during research, messaging discovery or ongoing optimization.

See hourly vs fixed-price freelance work.

Retainers fit recurring writing needs

Ongoing content, email, campaign or optimization work may be structured as a monthly retainer.

The buyer should define what capacity or output the retainer includes and how priorities are handled when multiple requests compete for the same time.

Revision scope should be defined before comparing quotes

One copywriter may include one revision round while another includes several. One may treat a new positioning direction as additional scope while another prices more iteration into the original project.

See the revision policy template.

Buyer inputs can materially change the price

The assignment becomes easier to estimate when the buyer provides useful information before work begins.

Target audience
Offer details
Product information
Existing brand voice
Proof or testimonials
Competitor context
Clear approval process

Use the project brief guide and project brief template.

Specialization can justify a higher rate

A copywriter with relevant industry experience may require less ramp-up time and may recognize buyer, customer or compliance issues earlier.

The buyer should compare the value of relevant experience against the cost of bringing a generalist up to speed.

Compare quotes by scope, not only by price

Two quotes may look very different because one includes strategy and research while the other covers drafting only.

Same deliverable?
Same research responsibility?
Same strategy responsibility?
Same number of revision rounds?
Same implementation or handoff?
Same timeline?

A higher rate can still produce a lower project cost

A more experienced copywriter may work faster, require less revision or avoid weak messaging directions earlier.

That does not make higher rates automatically better. It means hourly rate alone is not enough to compare total project economics.

Do not buy performance guarantees disguised as copywriting fees

Copy can influence conversion, but performance also depends on traffic quality, offer strength, design, pricing, product-market fit and other variables outside the writer's control.

The buyer should evaluate process, evidence and reasoning rather than paying for unsupported certainty.

Budget for implementation if the copy does not end at the document

Some engagements end with a final document. Others require coordination with designers, developers, email systems or publishing workflows.

Clarify whether implementation support is included or priced separately.

Use the business objective to evaluate the quote

A useful copywriting quote should show what problem is being solved, what work is required and what assumptions affect the price.
