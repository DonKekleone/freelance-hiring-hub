Freelance Hiring Guide

Review the portfolio for evidence, not decoration.

A beautiful portfolio can still be irrelevant to your project. Look for work that demonstrates the specific judgment, execution and constraints your job requires.

Start with relevance

The strongest example is not always the flashiest one. A simpler project that closely matches your requirements can tell you more than an unrelated award-winning piece.

Ask what the freelancer actually did

What part of the project did you personally handle?
What was the original problem?
What constraints did the client give you?
What changed during the project?
What would you do differently now?

Look for consistency

One extraordinary example surrounded by weak work may deserve more investigation. Several relevant projects showing similar judgment provide stronger evidence.

Separate presentation skill from project skill

A portfolio is itself a marketing document. Evaluate the underlying work, not only how attractively the case study is presented.

The best portfolio question is often 'why?'People who did the work can usually explain why decisions were made, not simply describe what appears on screen.

Verify when the stakes justify it

For larger projects, check public work, references or client relationships where practical.
