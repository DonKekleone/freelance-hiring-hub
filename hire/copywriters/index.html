Hire Freelancers

Hire a copywriter for the commercial problem, not the word count.

Copywriters can work on landing pages, emails, ads, sales pages and positioning. Define what the writing needs to accomplish before comparing samples.

Start with the asset and commercial objective

A landing page, email sequence, advertisement, homepage and sales page solve different problems. Define what is being written and what the reader should do after reading it before comparing writers.

What asset is being written?
Who is the audience?
What is the offer?
What action should the reader take?
Where will the copy appear?
What existing material is available?
Who approves the final copy?
What deadline matters?

Make sure you need a copywriter rather than another writing specialty

Copywriting is generally tied to persuasion, conversion or positioning. If the main goal is education, publishing or search-focused informational content, a content writer or blog writer may be a better fit.

Highly technical documentation may call for a technical writer, while editing an existing draft may require a proofreader or editor.

Give the writer useful inputs

Copy is built from information. A writer can improve structure, language and persuasion, but cannot manufacture accurate customer insight, product facts or offer clarity that the buyer never provides.

Audience and customer profile
Product or service details
Offer and pricing
Positioning or differentiators
Customer research
Testimonials or proof
Existing brand voice
Competitor context
Objections the copy should address
Good copy is built from information. A writer cannot recover customer insight, offer clarity and positioning that the buyer never supplies.

Use the project brief template or the guide to writing a project brief before requesting serious proposals.

Decide how much research belongs in the assignment

Some copywriting projects begin with a complete research package. Others require the writer to interview customers, review competitors, analyze existing materials or extract information from the business owner.

Those are different scopes. Clarify whether research is provided, shared or entirely the writer's responsibility.

Evaluate samples by objective, not style alone

A sample can sound polished without proving that the writer understands the commercial task you are hiring for. Look for examples that resemble the channel, audience and objective of your project.

A writer with strong email work is not automatically the best choice for a long-form sales page, and a strong brand writer is not automatically a direct-response specialist.

Use the portfolio review guide and portfolio review checklist to evaluate samples more systematically.

Ask what the writer actually contributed

Published copy can involve strategists, editors, designers, founders and marketing teams. Ask what the freelancer personally handled and whether the sample changed substantially after their draft.

Did you write the first draft?
Did you conduct the research?
Did you shape the offer or positioning?
Was the copy edited by someone else?
Was performance data available?

Interview for commercial reasoning

The strongest conversation is usually not about clever phrases. Ask how the writer thinks about the audience, offer, objections, proof and desired action.

What information would you want before writing?
What looks unclear about the offer?
What objections do you expect from the audience?
How would you approach research?
What would make this project difficult?
How do you handle conflicting feedback?

See the freelancer interview guide and interview questions template.

Define what strategy is included

Copywriting and marketing strategy can overlap, but they are not automatically the same service. One writer may expect the buyer to provide positioning and offer structure, while another may include strategic work in the engagement.

Clarify whether the freelancer is responsible only for the copy itself or also for research, positioning, messaging architecture, funnel structure or offer development.

Compare rates only after the scope is comparable

A quote for a landing page based on a complete brief is not comparable to a quote that includes interviews, competitive research, positioning and several rounds of revision.

Compare what each proposal actually includes before deciding that one writer is expensive or cheap.

For budgeting, see freelance copywriter rates, how much to pay a freelancer and the project budget calculator.

Choose hourly or fixed pricing based on the work

Fixed pricing can fit clearly defined assets such as a landing page or email sequence. Hourly pricing may make more sense when research, strategic work or ongoing revisions are difficult to predict.

See hourly vs fixed-price freelance work for the tradeoffs.

Define revision boundaries before drafting starts

Copy projects become unstable when strategy changes midway through the work or several stakeholders provide contradictory feedback.

How many revision rounds are included?
Who consolidates feedback?
What counts as a revision?
What counts as a new direction?
How are major strategy changes handled?
How are additional revisions priced?

Use the revision policy template and see what to do when revisions keep expanding.

Separate editing from a new strategic direction

Changing wording, tightening a section or correcting tone can be a normal revision. Replacing the offer, target audience or positioning after the draft may effectively create a new assignment.

A written scope of work helps define the baseline. If the project keeps expanding, see the guide to scope creep.

Give feedback against the brief

Feedback such as “I don't like it” gives the writer very little to work with. Explain which requirement is not being met, which audience reaction feels wrong or which claim does not match the offer.

Specific feedback tied to the brief usually produces better revisions than editing sentence by sentence without explaining the underlying issue.

See how to give better freelancer feedback.

Do not treat performance claims as guarantees

Copy can affect conversion, but the final result also depends on traffic quality, offer strength, pricing, design, product-market fit, implementation and measurement.

A writer may discuss prior outcomes or testing experience, but buyers should be cautious about guaranteed commercial results that depend on factors outside the writer's control.

Clarify ownership and usage rights

The engagement should make clear what the buyer is purchasing and when rights transfer. This matters especially when the copy will be used in advertising, published commercially or adapted across multiple channels.

Review intellectual property considerations before closing the project.

Plan the final handoff

Copy delivery may include more than a document full of text. Depending on the project, the buyer may need final approved copy, alternate headlines, annotations, research notes, source references or messaging documentation.

Final approved copy
Headline or subject-line variants where agreed
Supporting research or references
Notes needed by designers or developers
Messaging or voice guidance where included
Confirmation of final revision status

Use the project handoff checklist when the engagement includes multiple assets or supporting materials.

Watch for copywriting warning signs

No single issue proves that a writer is a poor fit, but buyers should look closely when a candidate asks almost nothing about the audience or offer, cannot explain their contribution to samples, promises guaranteed conversion results or provides a proposal that ignores the brief.

Review freelancer red flags and how to verify a freelancer.

A practical copywriter hiring sequence

Define the asset and commercial objective.
Clarify the audience, offer and desired action.
Decide what research the writer must perform.
Review relevant copy samples.
Interview the strongest candidates.
Clarify whether strategy is included.
Compare proposals against the same scope.
Agree on revisions and approval responsibility.
Review drafts against the brief.
Complete ownership and final-copy handoff.

Direct copywriting engagements can also use structured freelancer payments when the buyer wants funding tied to defined delivery.
