Project triage

When something breaks, identify the failure before reacting.

Freelance projects can fail for different reasons that look similar from the outside. A late delivery may be a timeline problem, a scope problem, a communication problem or evidence that the work itself is not progressing.

Start with four questions

What was actually agreed?
What has been delivered so far?
What changed after the agreement?
What outcome is still realistically recoverable?

Preserve the evidence

Keep the brief, messages, milestone terms, files, invoices and relevant approvals organized. Clear records make it easier to distinguish misunderstanding from missed obligations.

Classify the problem

Timing: Missed Deadline → Scope: Work Doesn't Match the Brief → Quality: Bad Work → Budget: Project Over Budget → Scope Creep → Communication: Freelancer Disappeared →

Repair, pause or replace

Not every problem requires ending the engagement. Some can be fixed with a clarified scope, revised milestone or better feedback. Others justify pausing further work or replacing the freelancer. Make that decision after identifying what is actually failing.

Recover the project deliberately

Diagnose first. Then decide whether to repair, pause or replace.

Different freelance failures require different responses. Before taking action, identify what changed, what remains usable and what the original agreement actually required.

Document the current state

Save the brief, milestone terms, messages, approvals, invoices, files and access details before changing the engagement. A clean record makes it easier to separate a misunderstanding from a missed obligation.
