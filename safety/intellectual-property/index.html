Freelance Hiring Safety

Do not wait until delivery to ask who owns the work.

Ownership, licensing and source-file expectations should be discussed before the project begins, especially when the work will become a business asset.

Ownership is not always automatic

Rules can vary by contract, jurisdiction and type of work. Buyers should avoid assuming that payment alone settles every intellectual-property question.

Clarify the important items

Who owns the final deliverable?
When does ownership transfer?
Are editable or source files included?
Are third-party assets used?
What licenses apply to fonts, images, music or software?
Can the freelancer display the work in a portfolio?
Are there restrictions on reuse?

Third-party assets need separate attention

A freelancer cannot necessarily transfer rights they do not own. Licensed images, fonts, music, plugins and stock assets may have their own terms.

Ownership language should match the real business need.A buyer commissioning a core brand asset may need different rights than someone purchasing a one-time illustration.

Use professional legal advice when the stakes justify it

For valuable intellectual property, regulated work or unusual ownership arrangements, qualified legal advice can be worth the cost.
