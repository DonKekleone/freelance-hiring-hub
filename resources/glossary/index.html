Freelance Hiring Reference

Freelance hiring terms without the jargon fog.

A practical glossary of the words buyers encounter when sourcing, hiring, paying and managing freelancers.

Acceptance criteria

The conditions a deliverable must satisfy before the buyer considers it complete or approved.

Advance payment

Money paid before some or all of the agreed work has been completed.

Brief

A document or message that explains the project objective, deliverables, requirements, constraints and context.

Buyer

The person or business purchasing freelance work.

Change request

A proposed change to the agreed work, timeline, requirements or deliverables after the project has started.

Deliverable

A specific item, asset, result or completed piece of work the freelancer is required to provide.

Dependency

Something the project requires before another task or milestone can continue, such as buyer approval, content, access or a third-party system.

Discovery

An early project stage used to clarify requirements, investigate uncertainty or determine the correct approach before full production begins.

Escrow

A payment arrangement where funds are held until agreed conditions for release are satisfied.

Fixed-price project

An engagement where defined work is completed for an agreed project price rather than being billed purely by time.

Freelancer

An independent worker who provides services to clients or buyers without being hired as a traditional employee for that engagement.

Handoff

The final transfer of completed files, source materials, access, documentation and other assets needed to use or maintain the work.

Hourly project

An engagement where the freelancer bills based on time worked.

Intellectual property

Legal rights associated with creative, technical or other original work. Ownership and licensing terms can vary by agreement and jurisdiction.

License

Permission to use intellectual property under specified conditions without necessarily transferring ownership.

Milestone

A project stage tied to a meaningful deliverable, review point or completion condition.

Minimum viable scope

The smallest useful version of the project that still produces a meaningful result or proves an important assumption.

Portfolio

A collection of work examples used to demonstrate a freelancer's experience, quality and relevant skills.

Proposal

A freelancer's response describing how they would approach the work, what they would deliver, expected timing and price.

Quote

A stated price or estimated cost for defined work.

Rate

The price used to calculate payment, such as an hourly, daily or per-unit amount.

Retainer

An ongoing payment arrangement used to reserve recurring freelance capacity or services.

Revision

A change to existing work intended to bring it closer to the agreed brief or scope. A major change in direction may instead be new scope.

Review window

The period the buyer has to inspect submitted work and provide approval, rejection or revision feedback.

Scope

The agreed boundaries of the project: what work is included, what is excluded and what the freelancer is expected to deliver.

Scope creep

Expansion of project requirements without corresponding changes to budget, schedule or agreement.

Scope of work

A written description of project deliverables, responsibilities, exclusions, schedule and completion requirements.

Source files

The editable or underlying working files used to create the final deliverable.

Statement of work

A formal project document that defines work to be performed, deliverables, responsibilities, timing and other engagement terms.

Subcontractor

A third party hired by the freelancer or primary provider to perform part of the project.

Time-and-materials

A pricing model where the buyer pays for actual time worked and, where applicable, project expenses or materials.

Turnaround time

The amount of time expected between a request, project start or submission and delivery of the work.

Usage rights

The rights granted to use a piece of work in specified ways, such as commercial use, advertising, distribution or modification.

Vetting

The process of evaluating and verifying a freelancer before increasing financial, operational or access exposure.

Work-for-hire

A legal concept that can affect who owns certain commissioned work. Its meaning and applicability depend on jurisdiction and the type of engagement.

Work sample

An example of completed work used to evaluate whether a freelancer's skills are relevant to the project.

Clear terminology reduces avoidable disagreement. When a term affects price, ownership, payment or completion, define what it means in the actual project instead of assuming both sides use it the same way.
